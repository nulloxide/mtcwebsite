Why Most Asset Managers Fly Blind

  • Fragmented data scattered across servicers, banks, and counterparties
  • Delayed visibility — risk reports arrive days or weeks after the damage
  • Manual reconciliation creates bottlenecks that don't scale
  • No independent verification — servicer data taken at face value
Most asset managers in our space depend on monthly servicer reports they can't independently validate. Data sits in different systems — loan tapes from the servicer, bank statements from the custodian, legal docs from counsel — and reconciling them is manual, slow, and error-prone. By the time a monthly report shows a problem, you've already lost weeks of response time.

Two things we want to highlight here. First, we don't wait for data after closing. During deal negotiations, our team runs preliminary analytics on the data we receive — loan tapes, payment tapes, bank statements, collateral records. During diligence, we do full data integration. The aim is that by closing, we have the monitoring infrastructure and data integration as ready as possible. After signing there are still things that need to dynamically change and be implemented, but the foundation is in place. Second, we don't take servicer data at face value. We rebuild core business logic — payment waterfalls, DPD calculations, outstanding principal, interest accrual, borrowing base — independently from the raw data. Then we compare our numbers to the servicer's. See this example? The servicer says 45 days past due, but when we re-derive from actual payment records, it's 52. That 7-day discrepancy triggers an alert and we investigate immediately — not at the next quarterly review.

How We Organize Data

BRONZE → SILVER → GOLD
  • Bronze — Raw data exactly as received. Immutable, checksummed, auditable
  • Silver — Cleansed, validated, deduplicated, PII-masked
  • Gold ≠ Dashboards — Gold is enriched data: DPD calculations, borrowing base logic, covenant checks, cross-dataset joins
  • Everything is regenerable from raw — we can always go back to source

Press → to explore the interactive architecture diagram

This is the Medallion Architecture — Bronze, Silver, Gold. Industry standard pattern, but our implementation is specific to private credit. When we say Gold, we don't mean a dashboard. Gold is the enriched, verified dataset where our proprietary business logic lives — DPD calculations, borrowing base formulas, covenant compliance checks, cross-dataset joins between loan tapes, bank statements, and legal documents. Dashboards are just one consumption layer on top. The real value isn't the chart — it's the verified, enriched data underneath. And everything is fully regenerable from the raw Bronze data at any time.
Medallion Architecture — hover over stages for details
Here's the full architecture. Sources on the left — loan management systems, banks, payment processors, legal docs — flowing in through various formats and integrations. Bronze is raw capture — the data exactly as we received it, stored permanently so we always have a record. Silver is where we clean and standardize — making sure data types are consistent, removing duplicates, protecting sensitive information. Think of it as making sure all the data speaks the same language regardless of where it came from. Then enrichment — this is where our proprietary logic lives. DPD calculations, outstanding principal, borrowing base, cross-dataset joins between different sources. Gold is the curated layer — ready for analysis, powering dashboards and investor reports. Notice the quarantine boxes — nothing is silently dropped. If data doesn't pass validation, it gets flagged for human review. And at the bottom — fully regenerable from raw. If we ever question a number, we trace it back to the source.

Catching Problems Before They Become Losses

  • Collection shortfalls surface days before servicer reports arrive
  • DPD migration patterns detected through daily monitoring — buckets shifting right is a signal
  • Cash velocity changes flag operational issues at the servicer level
  • Automated alerts trigger when metrics breach configurable thresholds

🔴 Example: 2% collection shortfall in week 2

By the time the monthly servicer report confirms it, we've already investigated, identified the root cause, and engaged the servicer with specific delinquent accounts.

This is where all the infrastructure earns its keep. Because we process data daily, we detect issues days or weeks before the monthly servicer report arrives. The example on screen — a two percent collection shortfall in week two. Most managers wouldn't know until the monthly report. We see it in real time. Let me show you what this looks like in practice.
Early Warning System — use the timeline scrubber to see degradation
This dashboard tells the story visually. Start at January on the left — everything looks healthy. Collections tracking scheduled, DPD buckets stable, borrowing base steady. Move to mid-February — the collection line starts falling behind. It's subtle, maybe one to two percent, but the system sees it. Continue to late February — cash velocity drops, DPD buckets start shifting. By early March it's clear — collections five percent behind, borrowing base declining. And look at the alert timeline at the bottom — our system started flagging this on February 16th. The difference between seeing this on February 16th and waiting until the March 31st servicer report is six weeks. In private credit, six weeks of advance warning is the difference between adjusting an advance rate and taking a loss.
